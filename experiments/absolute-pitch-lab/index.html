Landmark training

One note learned properly beats twelve learned vaguely. You start by deciding only whether a tone is C or not, then add notes that are maximally far apart before filling in the gaps. Feedback is immediate here, and the scramble still runs between trials so you can't chain one answer off the last.

What the numbers mean. Chance is 8.3%. Genuine absolute pitch usually looks like 90%+, fast, stable across timbre and register, with errors landing an octave off rather than a semitone off. Learned or partial AP looks like 40–70%, slower, much better on piano and on white keys, with errors clustered one or two semitones away — that clustering is the signature of anchoring to something and counting from it.

On the training. Adults do measurably improve at naming isolated pitches; what has never been reliably produced in an adult is the involuntary automaticity. Expect a real gain that is narrower and more effortful than the real thing, and that fades without maintenance. Re-screen every couple of weeks to see what actually stuck.
